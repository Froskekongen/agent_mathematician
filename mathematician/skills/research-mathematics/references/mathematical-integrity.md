# Shared Mathematical Integrity

Use this reference in every mathematical skill. It keeps intuition fast without
blurring what has actually been shown. It is not a proof protocol; each skill
defines its own completion rule.

Apply these rules to any detail that could change a claim, recommendation,
status, or the reader's mental model.

Use these rules behind the scenes. Build mathematical documents around
questions, ideas, examples, arguments, and conclusions. Put checklists,
workflow labels, digests, search logs, and database details in a short technical
note only when the reader needs them.

## Fidelity: preserve the mathematics

State important objects, maps, domains, quantifiers, assumptions, and
conventions precisely enough for the task. Distinguish the literal statement,
intended meaning, and any repaired or restricted version. Name competing
versions. A change to a definition, assumption, or conclusion creates a new
version.

Prose, formulas, diagrams, sources, and executable or formal encodings are
different representations. When moving between them, explain how they
correspond and what does not carry over.

## Warrant: match claims to evidence

When the role of an important part could be unclear, label it: definition,
imported result, deduction, checked example, counterexample, computation,
heuristic, analogy, conjecture, or proof sketch. State what it supports and over
what scope.

Match each conclusion to its evidence:

- A checked example may reveal a mechanism. It directly establishes that case,
  not a general pattern.
- A checked counterexample refutes the claim whose assumptions it satisfies and
  whose conclusion it contradicts.
- A computation supports only what was encoded and checked.
- An analogy or heuristic can explain, guide, or predict. It does not settle an
  unresolved claim.
- A failed search shows only that no defect was found within the searched scope.

Check any calculation, example, source use, or translation that supports a
conclusion. Use an imported theorem only when its exact statement, assumptions,
and conventions match the current case.

## Recoverable intuition: connect ideas to exact mathematics

Use mental models, pictures, examples, and metaphors freely. Connect each
important intuition to the exact objects and relations it describes. For an
analogy, say what carries over and where it breaks. A simple example and a
boundary case are quick checks against a misleading picture.

If an unresolved claim relies on a heuristic, name the smallest exact task
needed to justify that step. This **conversion obligation** might be stating a
missing definition, proving a lemma, matching an exact source, or extending a
check to the untested scope. Naming it does not complete it.

## Calibration: report the right level of certainty

Use language that matches the evidence and the active skill's completion rule.
Keep mathematical status separate from plausibility, confidence, usefulness,
workflow choice, explanatory value, and provenance. Explaining, rewriting,
translating, or consolidating a result does not change its mathematical status.

Check each claim in proportion to its role. Routine local claims may need only a
local check. If the evidence falls short, give the strongest supported
conclusion and state what remains open.
