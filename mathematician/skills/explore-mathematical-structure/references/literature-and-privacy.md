# Literature and Privacy Protocol

Read this reference when terminology is needed to proceed, rediscovery risk
affects resource allocation, the user requests prior art or novelty, or a
candidate is about to receive substantial investment because it appears new.

## Set the search mode

For sensitive unpublished work, record one mode before external search:

- `EXACT` — search the concrete objects, claims, and terminology;
- `ABSTRACTED` — search a generalized or redacted structural description;
- `NONE` — keep the exploration local.

Ask the user when privacy expectations or the permissible abstraction are
unclear and consequential.

## Use cold and warm passes

Run a cold pass before detailed anchoring when independent candidate
generation matters. Produce only a bounded set of hypotheses,
representations, or questions; preserve their pre-search form.

Run a warm pass when established terminology, exact prior results, or
novelty risk matters. Search for:

- equivalent constructions and alternative representations;
- known invariants, classifications, and canonical examples;
- counterexamples and boundary results;
- theorems with their exact hypotheses;
- cross-domain analogues;
- prior formulations of apparently new directions.

Search sources appropriate to the mathematical claim. Preserve exact
citations, queries or source scope, and the imported statement with its
hypotheses.

## Reconcile without overstating novelty

Classify a candidate as:

- known under the same formulation;
- known under another representation;
- a transfer or synthesis of known ideas;
- apparently distinct within the searched scope;
- unresolved because coverage is incomplete.

Use “no prior art found in the searched sources” rather than an unqualified
novelty claim. Distinguish `SOURCE-LOCATED` from `SOURCE-APPLICABLE`: the
latter requires checking that the source's objects, assumptions, and scope
match the current formalism.
